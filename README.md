# Retail_Store_Case_Study

## Overview

This repository presents a SQL case study in a retail context. It focuses on practical analytical tasks commonly encountered in retail business intelligence and customer analytics. The objective is to translate real-world business questions into SQL logic using a simple customer-orders schema.

The exercises strengthen foundational SQL concepts such as joins, group-by aggregation, subqueries for comparison logic, and city-level performance summarization. Each question mirrors common reporting tasks like customer spend analysis, regional performance tracking, and revenue insights for stakeholders.

## Key Features

* **Retail-domain** SQL practice grounded in real business requirements
* Customer and order-level analysis aligned with marketing and sales use cases
* Examples of group aggregation, conditional filtering, and subquery-based comparison
* Compact schema designed to emphasize business logic and SQL reasoning
* Queries structured to run on PostgreSQL or MySQL with minimal adjustment

## Dataset

The case study uses a simple retail schema that includes customer information and order transactions.
* Customer profiles including city and signup date
* Order transactions with category and purchase amount
This structure reflects real retail systems and supports customer segmentation and revenue analysis.

## How to Use

1. **Clone** this repository.
2. **Load schema and sample data** into your SQL environment (PostgreSQL or MySQL).
3. **Run the SQL queries** associated with each business question
4. **Adapt to your SQL engine** as needed.
5. Modify and extend queries to explore additional retail insights, such as city-level averages, customer ranking, and product category performance

## Insights You Can Find

* City-level customer base and revenue distribution
* Customer spending behavior for marketing segmentation
* Identification of above-average spenders for loyalty targeting
* Core revenue and customer metrics for business stakeholder reporting

These exercises help develop practical SQL competency, especially in analyzing transactional retail datasets and producing decision-driven insights.
